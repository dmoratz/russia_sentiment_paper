MEDIUM VERSION (with methodological note):
------------------------------------------
Figure X. Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) illustrating the causal relationships
between media ownership, editorial decisions, and article sentiment. The
shaded node (Topic) represents a collider variable, as it is influenced by
both the treatment (Invasion) and editorial priorities set by ownership.
Conditioning on Topic would open a backdoor path through Ownership, which
motivates our regression discontinuity design that exploits the exogenous
timing of the invasion.


LONG VERSION (for supplementary materials):
-------------------------------------------
Figure X. Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) depicting the hypothesized causal
structure underlying media sentiment toward Russia. Media Ownership
influences both the editorial priorities and the overall tone/style of
coverage. Editorial priorities determine topic selection, which affects
journalist drafts that ultimately produce the full article text from which
sentiment is measured. The 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine serves as an
exogenous shock that directly affects topic salience.

Importantly, Topic (shaded gray) functions as a collider in this DAG: it
receives causal arrows from both Invasion (the treatment) and Editor Sets
Priorities (which is influenced by Ownership). Thicker arrows highlight
these incoming paths. Conditioning on a collider can induce spurious
associations between its causes, which would bias estimates of the invasion's
effect on sentiment. Our identification strategy addresses this by exploiting
the sharp discontinuity in coverage around the invasion date, rather than
controlling for topic directly in all specifications.
